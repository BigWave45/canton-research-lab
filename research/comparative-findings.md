# Comparative Grant-Winner Research: Initial Findings

**Status:** Preliminary desk research, 17 November 2026  
**Sample:** 34 funded projects across three programmes (15 Ethereum, 16 Stellar, 3 Uniswap).  
**Important:** This is an initial, purposive sample, not a statistically representative dataset. Most entries are award-announcement descriptions, not independently verified completed outputs.

## 1. Programme comparability

These programmes do not fund identical things.

- **Ethereum Foundation Academic Grants:** a defined academic research round. The 2024 round announced 41 projects, with a total budget of USD 1,786,137.48 across eight categories. The award announcement includes short project descriptions and named researchers/institutions.
- **Stellar Development Foundation Academic Research Grants:** academic research programme with quarterly recipient lists. The programme page specifies institutional eligibility and says awards go to qualifying academic institutions, not directly to students, consultants, for-profit businesses, or individuals without eligible PI status. This is a major difference from a community content grant.
- **Uniswap Foundation:** mixed portfolio of research, infrastructure, ecosystem and security grants. Some awards are substantial multi-phase projects with explicit deliverables and KPIs, not directly comparable to individual research publication awards.
- **Canton Community Research Grants:** newly launched discretionary content/research programme beginning in November 2026. No historical Canton Community Research Grant winner cohort is available yet from the launch announcement. External programmes are therefore analogues, not direct Canton precedent.

## 2. Early observations from the funded project sample

### A. The research question is specific

Examples:
- “Market Microstructure and Incentive Design for Perpetual Decentralized Exchanges” (Stellar).
- “Collusion Resistance For Transaction Fee Mechanisms” (Stellar).
- “Balancing Anonymity and Compliance in Financial and Other Transactions” (Ethereum).
- “Analytical Model for Analyzing Leader-based and DAG-based Protocols” (Stellar).

These titles identify a mechanism, a problem, or a comparison. They are more precise than broad themes such as “the future of institutional finance.”

### B. Interdisciplinary work is strongest when disciplines meet around one defined problem

Examples:
- Institutional economics + governance + proof-of-stake slashing policy (Ethereum).
- Cryptography + financial privacy + compliance (Ethereum).
- Market microstructure + incentive design + perpetual DEXs (Stellar).
- Financial mathematics + AMM mechanism design (Stellar).
- Distributed systems + analytical modelling (Stellar).

This supports our interdisciplinary approach, but does not prove interdisciplinary projects are more likely to be funded. It demonstrates that funders have supported work at these intersections.

### C. A reusable research artefact can be a major contribution

The Uniswap Foundation's Allium DEX Analytics Portal grant is a useful case. The grant announcement explains a concrete research bottleneck: fragmented, inconsistent DEX data made reliable analysis difficult. The grant was USD 480,000, with deliverables including at least 10 data standards and a public portal over 21 months.

The important lesson is not “build a portal.” It is that research infrastructure can unlock work by many other researchers. A standardised dataset may have leverage beyond a single paper.

### D. Research can move from theory to implementation

Doppler is described as a Uniswap v4 liquidity-bootstrapping protocol using an auction mechanism and dynamic bonding curve. The project page connects a defined market failure (poor initial pricing and value leakage) to a mechanism design and implementation.

This is an example of research and implementation being integrated. We must inspect the code and technical documentation before making claims about measured performance or actual impact.

### E. The researchers' prior expertise is visible in academic programmes

The public award records frequently name researchers affiliated with universities and established research groups. For example, the Stellar award for AMM design lists researchers whose published work includes financial contagion, decentralized finance, game theory, and financial mathematics.

This is an important distinction for our planning: academic grants may evaluate investigator track record and institutional eligibility. Canton’s community programme is not described as an academic grant and should not be assumed to use the same eligibility or selection model.

## 3. What is not established yet

The current sample does not establish:
- why each project was selected;
- whether a particular characteristic caused selection;
- which funded projects completed successfully;
- which outputs were adopted or cited;
- whether a solo independent researcher can compete on the same basis as an academic team;
- what Canton reviewers will prioritise beyond the public announcement.

We must not claim that this sample reveals a winning formula.

## 4. Strongest case studies for deeper inspection

1. **Allium DEX Analytics Portal (Uniswap):** explicit problem, data standards, public portal, phased deliverables, and a clear downstream research beneficiary.
2. **Doppler (Uniswap):** market problem translated into mechanism design and a reusable implementation.
3. **Market Microstructure and Incentive Design for Perpetual DEXs (Stellar):** strong quantitative-finance intersection; locate the research output and inspect methods.
4. **Automated Market Maker Design to Optimize Liquidity Providers' Returns (Stellar):** mathematical finance and market design; locate output.
5. **An Institutional Economic Analysis of Slashing Penalties (Ethereum):** institutional economics and protocol governance; locate output.
6. **Balancing Anonymity and Compliance (Ethereum):** privacy engineering linked to financial and legal requirements; locate output.

## 5. Canton-specific implication

Canton's own announcement explicitly welcomes original research and educational content on:
- tokenomics and network economics;
- technical architecture, privacy, interoperability and Daml;
- institutional adoption and financial workflows;
- ecosystem analysis and network activity.

It says the work should be accurate, original, informative and evidence-supported, useful to community understanding, and should reach and inform the community. It also says awards are discretionary, are not a contest or promise, and do not require favourable coverage.

The external grant sample suggests we should avoid a broad explanatory article. We should identify a precise Canton-relevant problem, produce evidence or a transparent model, and create an output that others can reuse.
